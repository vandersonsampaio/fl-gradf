# C.0 — correction of 2026-10-04 (complements `NOTAS.md`, which stays intact)

`NOTAS.md` is not edited: the hash in `NOTAS.sha256` covers the whole file and records the frozen state before the oracle ceiling. This note corrects the **interpretation** of §6.2 and adds the requested note. **The criterion (§5) and the pre-registered verdict (§6) do not change.**

## 1. §6.2's explanation of the negative gaps at α ≤ 0.1 was wrong

§6.2 attributed the negative gaps (methods under attack above the FedAvg-8 oracle ceiling at α ≤ 0.1) to two causes:
- removing the 2 clients would cost ~7–8 p.p., because they hold classes poorly represented in the others;
- several attacks would still carry part of the information in the attacker's data.

Both were refuted by later checks:

| check | result | source |
|---|---|---|
| Weight mass on attackers | ≈ 0 on `gaussian_noise`, `krum_collusion` and `trim_attack` (α 0.05 and 0.1): the attackers are excluded; there is no exploitation | `results/a0_analises/RESULTADO.md` (a); `results/c0b_espaco_h150/massa_atacantes_a0.1.csv` |
| Oracle code with an all-ones mask | exactly reproduces FedAvg-10 (\|Δ\| = 0): there is no bug | `results/c0b_espaco_h150/VERIFICACOES.md` §1 |
| Oracle-8 with **uniform** weighting | α 0.1, H = 150: 89.5%, equal to `sr_bin` under `gaussian_noise` (89.5%) and ≈ FedAvg-10 (89.4%); the sample-weighted oracle gives 85.0% | `VERIFICACOES.md` §3; `sensibilidade_oraculo_uniforme/` |

**Actual cause:**
- The framework's `FedAvgStrategy` weights **by sample size**. With α ≤ 0.1, sizes range from ~10² to ~10⁴ examples per client.
- *(Mechanism corrected on 2026-10-05.)* The C.0 metric is the mean of the accuracies on the 10 clients' test sets, which equals the accuracy on the **global MNIST test set, IID and class-balanced** (split into equal parts, 1000 examples per client; `data/download_datasets.py`; checked in B2.8s).
- With per-client label skew, the sample-weighted oracle gives too much weight to the classes of the large clients, and the aggregated model becomes unbalanced on a balanced test set. The best methods under attack exclude the attackers and weight the honest clients **uniformly**, which dilutes that bias, and that is why they beat it.
- Removing the 2 clients costs ~1.8 p.p. at α 0.1 (91.23 − 89.46) and ~2.5 p.p. at α 0.05 (90.44 − 87.92), at H = 150 and with both ceilings (FedAvg-10 and oracle-8) uniformly weighted. Not 7–8 p.p.

**The "attack oracle" suggested in §6.2 is not needed.** The appropriate reference ceiling is the **uniform oracle-8** (exclusion of the attackers + uniform weighting of the honest clients). It is a reference, **not an upper bound**: another weighting of the honest clients (e.g. class balancing) can beat it.

## 2. Consequence for the C.0 map

- The C.0 oracle ceiling (15 rounds, sample-size weighting) **underestimates** the ceiling at α ≤ 0.1. The 10 cells with a negative gap were not an "invalid ceiling" because of the attackers' data: they were the sample-weighted ceiling.
- C.0 also had two other defects:
  - **underestimated `sr_only` baseline:** `sr_only` (λ = 2, b = 0.5, soft) is the worst skeleton variant (B2.6);
  - **15 rounds without convergence:** the ceiling gains 6–9 p.p. up to H = 150 at α ≤ 0.1 (B2.7).
- **The valid remaining-headroom map is C0b's** (`results/c0b_espaco_h150/RESULTADO.md`): H = 150, skeleton variants and static rules, seeds 72–81.
  - **Pre-registered** (sample-weighted oracle): 0/19 cells with headroom against the best existing method.
  - **Sensitivity with the uniform oracle:** 1/19, `label_flipping` α 0.05: +2.8 p.p. (CI95 +2.2 to +3.4) against FLTrust.
